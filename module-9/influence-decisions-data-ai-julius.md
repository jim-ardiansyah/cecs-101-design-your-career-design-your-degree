# Assignment: Influence Decisions with Data and AI using Julius AI

## Purpose

Data and AI don't just describe reality. They shape what decisions get made, by whom, and how confidently. In this assignment you'll use **Julius AI**, a conversational data analysis tool, to simulate a real decision-making scenario. You'll feed it data, let it generate insights and recommendations, and then critically examine how those AI-generated outputs would actually influence a decision-maker, including where that influence could go wrong.

This is not a tutorial on how to use Julius AI. It's a critical thinking exercise that uses Julius AI as the case study.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1: Choose a Decision Scenario

Pick a realistic decision that a real person or organization would need to make using data. Choose one:

- A business deciding whether to launch a product based on sales or survey data
- A hospital or clinic deciding on a treatment protocol based on patient outcome data
- A city government deciding where to allocate a budget based on public data
- A hiring manager deciding between candidates using performance or screening data
- A school deciding on an intervention based on student performance data
- Your own scenario, approved by the instructor

You'll need a dataset, real or realistic. Good sources include Kaggle, a government open data portal, or a dataset provided by the instructor.

### Step 2: Let Julius AI Drive the Decision

Upload your dataset to Julius AI and interact with it the way a decision-maker actually would, in plain language, not as a data scientist. Ask it to:

1. Summarize what the data shows
2. Identify the top trend, driver, or recommendation
3. Make a recommendation for the decision you're facing
4. Justify that recommendation in its own words

Save your full chat transcript with Julius AI. You'll submit it along with your presentation.

### Step 3: Critically Examine the Influence (Written Analysis)

This is the core of the assignment. Answer the following in your report:

**A. What did the AI actually do?**
Separate what Julius AI calculated, a statistical fact, from what it recommended, a judgment call. AI tools often blend these together, so your job is to pull them apart.

**B. Where could this recommendation mislead a real decision-maker?**
- Did Julius AI pick the analysis method, chart type, or framing on its own? Would a different reasonable choice have told a different story?
- Is there a gap between correlation and causation in what it presented?
- What did it not ask about? Missing context, confounding variables, or data quality issues that a human analyst should have flagged.

**C. Would a non-technical decision-maker have caught the issue?**
Imagine someone with no data background reading only Julius AI's summary and recommendation, not the underlying data. What would they likely misunderstand, over-trust, or accept without question?

**D. Rerun it your way.**
Go back to Julius AI and ask at least one follow-up question designed to stress-test its own recommendation. For example: "what if we exclude outliers," "what's the sample size," "show me the confidence interval," or "what's the counterargument to this recommendation." Report how its answer changed, or didn't.

**E. Your verdict.**
Would you act on Julius AI's recommendation as-is? What would you need to see before you'd trust it enough to influence a real decision?
