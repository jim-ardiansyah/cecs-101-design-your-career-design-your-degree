# 48-Hour Tool Adoption Challenge
### Building Adaptability in the Age of AI

Technology doesn't wait for you to feel ready. New tools — especially AI-powered ones — show up constantly, and the people who thrive are the ones who can learn them quickly instead of avoiding them. This assignment puts you in that exact situation on purpose: you'll pick an unfamiliar tool, give yourself only 48 hours to get functional with it, and pay close attention to *how* you learn, not just *what* you learn.

---

## Learning Objective

By the end of this assignment, you will be able to:
- Learn a new, unfamiliar tool under a real time constraint
- Identify the specific strategies that help you adapt quickly
- Reflect honestly on the discomfort of not knowing something yet, and how you moved through it

---

## Step 1 — Choose Your Tool (Do this first, before the clock starts)

Select a tool or app you have **never used before**. It should be:

- **New to you** — not something you've dabbled in before
- **Ideally AI-related** (an AI writing tool, image generator, AI-powered productivity app, coding assistant, transcription tool, etc.) — though a non-AI tool is acceptable if you check with your instructor first
- **Free or accessible** without a paid subscription
- **Realistic in scope** — something you could plausibly learn the basics of in two days, not a professional-grade software suite that takes months to master

**Need ideas?** Ask your instructor for a list of suggested tools, or propose your own for approval before starting the clock.

## Step 2 — Set Your Goal

Before you start learning, decide what "success" looks like. Choose **one** of the following as your target for the end of the 48 hours:

- [x] **Complete a small task** using the tool (e.g., generate a short project, produce a piece of content, automate a simple process)
- [ ] **Teach a classmate** how to use the core features of the tool (in person, by video, or in writing)

Write a one-sentence description of your specific goal:

---

## Step 3 — Learn the Tool (Over the Next 48 Hours)

Dive in. Explore the tool, watch tutorials if needed, experiment, make mistakes, and figure it out. There's no single "right" way to do this — the point is to notice your *own* process as you go.

### Journal Requirement

Keep a short journal during the 48 hours. You don't need to write an essay each time — a few honest sentences per entry is enough. Make **at least 3 journal entries** across the two days (for example: one shortly after you start, one partway through, and one near the end).

For each entry, note:
- **What you tried** (what did you do in this session?)
- **What was confusing or frustrating** (where did you get stuck? What made you want to give up?)
- **What helped** (a tutorial, a friend, trial and error, reading the FAQ, something else?)
- **How you felt** (be honest — overwhelmed, curious, bored, excited, embarrassed, etc.)

**Journal Entry 1** — *(shortly after starting)*
- What I tried: To begin, I opened Obsidian, created a new vault, and labeled two note pages as my daily routine and my morning timing.
- What was confusing or frustrating: At this point, it was difficult for me to figure out where notes automatically save and how to work the file navigation sidebar.
- What helped: The neat layout of Obsidian helped me greatly, as it was very easy to discover how to create a new note and type on a clean page.
- How I felt: I was a bit cautious at first in order not to click on anything I did not want, but I quickly became comfortable with the layout due to the very clean and minimalist design of the application.

**Journal Entry 2** — *(partway through)*
- What I tried: Now that my notes have been created, I added a tag and bullet points along with the text itself to my first note, laying out my typical morning routine on weekdays.
- What was confusing or frustrating: The button for a tag was not initially clear to me, but after a bit of trial and error I was able to navigate my way around the notes page.
- What helped: The editing toolbar buttons were quick and easy, and after clicking on them once, they were very easy to remember.
- How I felt: This time around, I was much more comfortable with the application, and already starting to appreciate it as an upgrade over the Apple notes app.

**Journal Entry 3** — *(near the end)*
- What I tried: Near the end of my time, I finalized my vault by creating a second notes page in which I added another tag and bullet points before writing out my wake up times and first class times on these days.
- What was confusing or frustrating: I spent the most time deciding between a second notes page or one singular, larger notes page for my vault.
- What helped: The addition of a second notes page made each of the pages feel more simplified with only a few bullet points in each one.
- How I felt: At the end, I felt very satisfied with my mini Obsidian creation and confident that I can utilize it in the future for more complex tasks.

## Step 5 — Final Reflection (Write this after your 48 hours are up)

Write a reflection of **8–12 sentences** responding to the following:

- What made this tool difficult to learn at first? Be specific.
- Which strategy helped you adapt the *fastest* — and why do you think it worked?
- Did your feelings about the challenge change from the beginning to the end? How?
- What does this experience tell you about how you personally learn new things under pressure?
- How might this same approach help you the next time a new tool, app, or AI system shows up unexpectedly at school or work?

**My Reflection:**
At first, Obsidian was slightly difficult to learn due to the large number of shortcuts that are built into the app. In addition, there were many buttons and symbols that I did not recognize at first. For example, the button for a tag looked very similar to a price tag, which I had never seen on an application before. Therefore, the strategy that helped me adapt the fastest was simply to click on everything and see what happened. This trial and error type of approach worked very well, as I was not at risk of losing any important projects or anything, I was just learning my way around the application. I was not, however, ever frustrated with the application, as I viewed the entire process as a learning experience. Therefore, I maintained a positive outlook towards the challenge for the entirety of the 48 hours. I believe that this experience has shown me that when I am interested in something, I truly enjoy learning about it even if there happens to be a strict time deadline. In the future, I have confidence that I can use this same approach the next time I am forced to learn a new application in a short period of time. I am not afraid to make mistakes, and thus there is a high possibility that I will enjoy getting to know the new tool.
